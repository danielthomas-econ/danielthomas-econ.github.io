Daniel Johann Thomas

B.A. (Honors) Economics
St. Stephen’s College, University of Delhi

I am an economics undergraduate at St. Stephen’s College, University of Delhi, and will graduate in 2027. Years of tinkering with computers as a child eventually led me to teach myself Python, and along the way I grew fond of mathematics, especially its more abstract side, which has come to sit naturally alongside both economics and programming.

Most of my work is about information in markets. Economists have said at least since Hayek that prices carry information, yet it is surprisingly rare to ask how much, so my paper on prediction markets tries to measure it in bits, using markets whose outcomes are eventually revealed. My Nagpaul Fellowship paper, written under the guidance of Prof. Anant Singh, grew out of watching how differently people reacted as a politician’s claims became more misleading. It studies deception by a general producer that cannot tell how much its audience will notice, so the same results describe a firm misleading its customers as much as a politician misleading voters. A third paper, which draws on my experience growing up in Dubai, asks what Schelling’s model of segregation looks like once people have to pay rent to live beside the neighbors they want.

I am usually drawn in by a question before I think about how to answer it, and I would rather learn whatever method a question needs than look for questions that suit a method I already know. So far that has taken me through game theory, a 43‑million‑row panel of market prices and a simulation of a million households, and I expect the list to keep growing. Right now I am most interested in industrial organization, information economics and market design, and I am looking for positions starting in summer 2027.
